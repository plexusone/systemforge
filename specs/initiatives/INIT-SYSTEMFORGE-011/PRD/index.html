    PRD — Suite Organizations: Lifecycle, Claims, Collaborators, Real-Time Invalidation¶

    Initiative: INIT-SYSTEMFORGE-011 Status: Draft Home repo: github.com/plexusone/systemforge Design of record: ADR-006, ADR-007, ADR-008, ADR-009, ADR-010 Depends on: INIT-SYSTEMFORGE-007 (managed principals and manager relationships), INIT-SYSTEMFORGE-008 (single Principal root)

    Problem¶

    SystemForge models organizations, memberships, invites, managed principals, a marketplace and two authorization providers, but the lifecycle that ties them into something an application can build on is missing. Five accepted ADRs describe the gaps; this initiative implements them together because they share tables, claims and transactions.

    Organizations have no lifecycle (ADR-006). SystemAuth's social login creates a bare principal and never calls signup, so a person's first sign-in leaves them in no organization and every application needs an operator step before the person can do anything. Slugs can be changed freely: nothing remembers a freed slug, so a rename can be followed at once by someone else taking the old name, and links to the old slug break. A personal organization cannot become a team without creating a new organization and moving everything, and nothing enforces what makes a personal organization personal. The plan column on organizations is a placeholder from before the marketplace: set at creation, never consulted, and unable to describe an organization that holds several subscriptions.

    Applications keep their own membership tables (ADR-007). SystemAuth is meant to be the source of truth for memberships, roles and entitlements, but it emits none of them, so every application keeps its own table fed by operators or its own invite flow. In a deployment of several applications behind one SystemAuth, the same organization has to be created in each, a person has to be added to each separately, and removal from one does not remove from another. The marketplace records what an organization is entitled to, but applications cannot learn that from a login either.

    Access changes wait for token expiry (ADR-010). Claims make requests cheap because a relying party asks SystemAuth nothing per request; the cost is that removing someone from an organization is not seen until their token expires. For routine offboarding that is tolerable; for a contractor cut off during a dispute, a compromised account or an agent whose mission was revoked, it is not. Introspection on every request would reintroduce the network hop the claims model removed; shorter lifetimes shrink the window but never close it.

    Every grant is organization-wide (ADR-008). There is no way to give someone access to one resource — a project, a document, a site — without making them a member of the organization. Solo users need that for contractors, teams need it for outside reviewers, and personal organizations have no members at all. The substrate already has the pieces (relationships, vocabularies, lookups); the pattern should be defined once rather than rebuilt per application.

    Management is inferred from a missing email (ADR-009). A managed principal is currently a human whose email is null. That excludes children with a school-issued address, who still need a guardian in control, and adults managed by arrangement, whose manager's authority comes from a relationship they both accept. The email answers whether a person can be contacted and verified; the relationship answers who may act for them.

    Goals¶

    1. A working organization after one login. A first social login creates the principal and its personal organization in one transaction, with a slug derived from how the person arrived.
    2. Safe, reversible renames. A freed slug is held for seven days for its previous holder, resolves to its previous organization as an alias for that time, and the same rule applies to every kind of rename.
    3. Solo to team without moving data. A personal organization converts to a team keeping its ID, data and memberships; the person keeps their handle on a fresh personal organization.
    4. Entitlement per listing, not per organization. The plan column is removed and an entitlements summary derived from marketplace subscriptions and licenses takes its place.
    5. Memberships, application roles and entitlements as claims. The ID token, userinfo and access token carry an orgs claim; applications read it through relyingparty and keep no membership tables of their own.
    6. Access changes take effect within one request in the same process, within the polling interval on replicas, within back-channel delivery time across deployments, and within one token lifetime if every signal is lost.
    7. Collaborators on both authorization modes. Resource grants, ListResources and resource-targeted invites behave the same under rbac and rebac, verified by a shared conformance suite.
    8. Management is a relationship. IsManaged and the managed claim derive from active manager relationships; email decides only whether management may end.

    Non-Goals¶

    • Pricing and packaging policy: which tiers exist, whether there is a free tier, trial lengths. The marketplace expresses them; applications choose them.
    • Custom or organization-defined application roles, and teams as carriers of roles. The roles are the ones an application's vocabulary declares; teams are the first addition if per-person assignment becomes the problem.
    • Resource grants as claims. The orgs claim lists a collaborator's organizations; the resources they may access are the application's to look up.
    • Revocation of agent tokens. AAuth tokens carry their own cascading revocation keyed by jti; this initiative covers SystemAuth-issued tokens.
    • Moving a resource between organizations. An application that supports it must move the grants and the collaborator memberships they imply.
    • Deciding what a managed principal may do for itself. The platform supplies the relationship, the roles and the managed claim; the split is the application's vocabulary.
    • Any product-specific signup page, organization switcher or invite screen. SystemAuth's own pages and API are in scope; an application's user interface is not.

    Users and Stakeholders¶

    Who Needs
    A person signing in for the first time An organization that works at once, with a handle they recognize, no operator involved
    A solo user Contractors with access to one resource, not the organization; a path to a team that keeps their data and ID
    An organization administrator Invites and member screens that choose application roles per application; role changes that take effect now; a rename that does not break links
    An application on SystemForge Organizations, roles and entitlements from the login, keyed by a stable organization ID; a first-use provisioning hook; a one-line revocation cache; ListResources to filter queries for collaborators
    A suite of applications behind one SystemAuth One membership, one invite, one removal across every application; no per-application organization tables
    A manager of a managed principal Management that survives the principal gaining an email; an explicit, audited release
    A deployment operator A configured access-token lifetime, clock-skew margin, default entitlements and authz.mode, each documented with its default

    Requirements¶

    Organization lifecycle (ADR-006)¶

    • F1. SystemAuth's GitHub and Google login call signup.SignupOrGetExisting for a principal they have not seen; the principal and its personal organization are created in one transaction with the principal as owner. A later login resolves by provider identity first, then by verified email, and creates nothing.
    • F2. The personal slug is derived in one place, signup.SlugForLogin: the GitHub username; the local part of a Google email; for a managed principal, a manager-entered name or a generated slug that reveals nothing about the person; a numeric suffix on collision.
    • F3. A reserved-slug list (admin, api, o, login, oauth, well-known names and deployment-configured product names) is refused by SlugAvailable.
    • F4. Every slug change frees the old slug into a seven-day cooling-off period during which only the releasing principal, or an organization that principal owns, may take it. The freed slug resolves to its previous organization as an alias until reassigned or expired; lookups consult aliases after exact matches. The rule applies to personal and team organizations and in every direction.
    • F5. A personal organization has exactly one member, its owner. AddMember and invites are refused for personal organizations except for role collaborator; TransferOwnership is refused.
    • F6. ConvertToTeam keeps the organization's ID, data and memberships, sets its type to team, takes a free team slug chosen at conversion, and creates a new personal organization with the person's handle for the owner, in one transaction.
    • F7. The plan column is removed from sf_organizations, the organization service inputs and list filter, API responses, the SCIM default and the Plan* constants. The organization API gains an entitlements summary derived from the marketplace.

    Memberships and entitlements as claims (ADR-007)¶

    • F8. Application roles are stored per (principal, organization, application) in sf_app_roles, validated against the application's AppVocabulary ladder. Owners hold admin in every entitled application implicitly; admins and members hold only what is assigned, none by default; collaborators hold none. Removal from the organization removes every application role.
    • F9. The applications an organization may assign roles in derive from its active subscriptions and licenses per listing, mapped to registered application identifiers, plus deployment-configured default entitlements. A deployment with no entitlements at all logs a warning at startup. A seat-licensed application counts its sf_app_roles rows against the license.
    • F10. The ID token and userinfo carry orgs: a list of {id, slug, name, type, role, apps} with apps a map of application identifier to application role. JWT access tokens carry the compact {id, role, apps}. A token carries at most 50 organizations, personal first then by most recent membership, with orgs_truncated: true when any were omitted; GET /userinfo/memberships returns the full paginated list.
    • F11. relyingparty reads memberships from claims by default (ClaimsMemberships), from the memberships endpoint when truncated or when only the compact form is present (RemoteMemberships), and keeps LocalMemberships for development only. Applications get a first-use provisioning hook keyed by organization ID.
    • F12. Invite creation and acceptance, and member management, carry per-application role selection with none preselected; roles change per application without touching the membership. SystemAuth's pages and API expose both.

    Real-time invalidation of access (ADR-010)¶

    • F13. Every access change — membership removal, organization or application role change, a grant reduction, deactivation, session revocation — is recorded in sf_access_changes as (principal_id, organization_id or null, changed_at, reason) inside the transaction that makes the change.
    • F14. A role or membership change no longer revokes refresh tokens; the next refresh carries the reduced claims. Deactivation and explicit session revocation still revoke.
    • F15. The access-token lifetime defaults to 10 minutes, is configurable, and may not exceed 15 minutes.
    • F16. relyingparty keeps a revocation cache keyed by principal (and organization when scoped) and accepts a token only if `iat >= changed_at
    • margin, with a configurable clock-skew margin defaulting to 30 seconds and floored at 15. A rejected token is answered as expired so the client refreshes. Entries expire atchanged_at + maximum lifetime + margin`.
    • F17. In one process the cache is shared memory, written in the recording transaction. Replicas poll sf_access_changes every one to two seconds. Separate deployments receive an OIDC Back-Channel Logout token carrying an access_change event, with retry and logging.
    • F18. After a restart a relying party replays GET /access-changes?since= before serving and treats every token as suspect until replay succeeds.

    Resource-scoped collaborators (ADR-008)¶

    • F19. The base schema gains a collaborator relation and a list permission on organization; collaborators have no view, edit or manage. collaborator is a membership role. The viewer/contributor/maintainer resource-role ladder is reserved in docs/authz-conventions.md.
    • F20. authz gains GrantResourceRole, RevokeResourceRole, ListResourceGrants and ListResources, implemented by rbac through sf_resource_grants and by rebac/spicedb through relationships and LookupResources. The first grant to a non-member creates a collaborator membership; revoking the last grant removes it.
    • F21. Invites may target a resource (type, ID, role); acceptance creates the collaborator membership and the grant in one transaction.
    • F22. Applications configure authz.mode: rbac | rebac and, for rebac, an engine. The deprecated authz/simple and authz/spicedb forwarders are scheduled for removal in the next major release.

    Management is a relationship (ADR-009)¶

    • F23. IsManaged and the managed claim derive from active manager relationships. ErrNotManaged means "has no active manager"; ErrLastManager keeps its meaning and is reworded to say the last manager of a principal without a verified email cannot be removed.
    • F24. Creating a managed principal accepts an optional email, verified like any other before it counts for release.
    • F25. Release removes the last manager of a principal with a verified email and clears the managed state in one transaction.
    • F26. A self-managed human enters management only by accepting a manager invitation, a new invite kind carrying the manager role.
    • F27. Slugs for managed principals keep the manager-entered or generated form whether or not an email exists; an email's local part is never used.

    Non-Functional¶

    • N1. Every operation that spans tables — signup, rename, conversion, invite acceptance, grant plus membership, release, access-change recording — is one database transaction.
    • N2. The per-request cost of the revocation check is a map lookup; the cache holds only changes within one token lifetime plus the margin.
    • N3. Both authorization modes pass the same providertest suite for organization roles, application roles, resource grants and lookups.
    • N4. Existing conformance suites pass; tests that added members to personal organizations use team organizations.
    • N5. golangci-lint reports no findings; the mkdocs strict build passes.
    • N6. Every manager action, role assignment, grant and release is audited with the acting principal.

    Success Criteria¶

    • A new person signs in with GitHub and lands in a personal organization whose slug is their GitHub username; signing in again creates nothing.
    • Renaming a personal organization, giving the old slug to a team, and reclaiming it within seven days each succeed; a stranger taking it within seven days is refused; links to the old slug resolve throughout.
    • An application that keeps no membership table provisions itself for an organization on first use from the orgs claim, shows the person only the applications they hold a role in, and sees an owner as admin everywhere.
    • Removing a person from an organization is rejected by the application's next request in the same process, with a token-expired error that leads to a refresh carrying the reduced claims, and the person stays signed in to their other organizations.
    • A collaborator granted viewer on one resource sees that resource and no other, under rbac and under rebac, and the organization appears in their switcher with role collaborator.
    • A managed principal with a school-issued email keeps its guardian; releasing it is an explicit, audited call that succeeds only when the email is verified.

    Risks¶

    Risk Mitigation
    Dropping plan breaks consumers that read it The column is unconsulted today; the entitlements summary ships in the same item, and the change is called out in the release notes
    A missed access-change site leaves a stale claim honored The recording call is in the service transaction, not the handler; the list of change sites is enumerated in the TRD and tested one by one; the token lifetime remains the bound
    Clock skew between SystemAuth and a separate relying party The margin, its floor and the expiry rule are specified in the TRD; in-process both timestamps come from one clock
    The two authorization modes drift providertest grows cases for every grant and lookup operation and runs against both
    Slug cooling-off is gamed or locks a handle forever Reservations expire; only the releasing principal or their organizations may reclaim; reserved names are refused up front
    Deployment with no entitlements refuses every organization Startup warning; default entitlements are a documented configuration

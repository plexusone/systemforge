    ADR-011: Lifecycle States, Data Classification and Privacy Rights¶

    Status: Proposed Deciders: @grokify Relates to: ADR-005 and ADR-009 (managed principals; this ADR amends the release decision of ADR-009), ADR-006 (organization lifecycle), ADR-010 (access changes), ADR-012 (enforcement and the semantics of a hold). Updates: docs/design/architecture/managed-principals.md, docs/design/architecture/organizations.md, a new docs/design/architecture/data-governance.md.

    Context¶

    SystemForge holds personal data for every product built on it, and several decisions so far assumed an account either exists or is "deactivated". Four needs do not fit that:

    • Hiding without destroying. An account that should disappear from customer-facing applications, such as a departed member or a closed profile, while staff can still see it and restore it.
    • Deleting without breaking references. Principals are referenced by memberships, audit records, work items and grants. Deleting the row would either cascade through those or fail on foreign keys.
    • Privacy rights. GDPR and similar regimes give a data subject access, portability, rectification, restriction and erasure. These are cross-cutting: every product stores data about the same person. They belong in the platform layer so each product does not reimplement them, and so there is one place that knows which columns hold personal data.
    • Dead and unwanted accounts at scale. A free tier accumulates accounts that were abandoned and accounts created by spammers. Soft deleting every one and keeping a tombstone is the wrong answer for spam, which should leave no personal data and no row behind, and abandoned accounts should not be kept indefinitely, which the storage-limitation principle also requires. Versioned entities, whose history grows with every edit, add unbounded growth of their own.

    Managed principals add a related question. ADR-009 says releasing a principal removes its managers at once. In practice the end of a management arrangement is a process: the manager may need to finish handover, and the record of the arrangement should be editable and, like a release object on a code host, correctable or removable before it is final.

    Decision¶

    Lifecycle states¶

    1. A principal and an organization have a lifecycle state: provisional, active, archived or deleted. Transitions: provisional → active (graduation, decision 19), active ⇄ archived, and active or archived → deleted. Deleted is terminal. A subject may also be purged (decision 20), which removes it and is not a state it stays in.
    2. Archived is reversible and means "not for customers to see":
    3. it is excluded from listings, search, mentions and pickers in customer-facing applications;
    4. platform administrators and the owners' admin tooling can still list, read and restore it;
    5. it cannot sign in at all, so that "archived" has one clear meaning, and its sessions are revoked when it is archived (an access change, ADR-010); an archived organization's members lose access to it through their claims; data export for an archived subject is performed by an administrator;
    6. memberships, grants and history are preserved.
    7. Deleted is a soft delete. The row stays so references hold, and every column classified as personal data (below) is erased according to its erasure strategy. The identifier is replaced by a non-reversible tombstone, credentials, external identities, sessions, consents and claims are removed, and memberships and relationships are deactivated. Deletion of personal data is irreversible; the tombstone is not.
    8. The delete transition never removes the row. deleted keeps a tombstone so references hold. Removing rows is a separate operation, purge (decision 20), allowed only where nothing depends on them and nothing forbids it. An application that must remove tombstoned rows after a retention period does so through the platform's purge, not its own job.
    9. Transitions require authority and are recorded: an actor with the account-management permission (or a platform administrator), an access-change reason for each transition (ADR-010), and an audit record that holds no personal data.

    Data classification¶

    1. Every persisted field is classified, on two independent axes.
    2. Visibility is public or internal. public may be shown to application users, subject to the application's own authorization. internal is system data not shown to application users: flags, counters, security stamps, internal references and operational state.
    3. Tags, each on or off: pii (personal data about an identifiable person) and secret (credentials, tokens, keys, anything whose disclosure grants access). The axes are independent: a display name is public and pii; a login record's IP address is internal and pii; a password hash is internal and secret. One rule ties them: secret implies internal, and validation rejects a secret field marked public. Visibility is never defaulted: an unclassified field is an error, not a public one. A field also carries optional attributes: an erasure strategy (clear, tombstone, hash, delete_row, shred or retain with a stated reason) and an export flag. An entity carries the subject key (how a row maps to a data subject), whether it is in privacy scope, whether it can be placed under hold, whether it is versioned, and a retention note.
    4. The model is a separate, generic module, and the classification is metadata in Go next to the schema. The catalog model, its validation and its audience filtering live in github.com/grokify/datacatalog, a public, dependency-free module that other tools (a query guard such as guardsql, documentation generators, a future management application) consume without depending on SystemForge or on an ORM. SystemForge adds the pieces that are specific to it: Ent annotations on each field of its own schemas, collected into a catalog by a small dataclass package, and the orchestration of export, erasure and holds. Applications annotate their own entities the same way and register them. A test fails when a field has no classification, so a new column cannot ship unclassified.
    5. The catalog is the single source for governance tooling. It drives export and erasure (below), log redaction, response filtering (a serialization helper omits every internal field unless the caller asks for a staff or system view), and query gating: a query guard such as guardsql blocks internal and secret columns for customer queries always, and pii columns unless the profile allows them. The guard adopts the catalog's own predicate, so there is one definition of what a customer query may touch.

    Privacy rights¶

    1. SystemForge provides the operations; applications decide policy.
    2. Access and portability: Export(subject) returns the subject's records from every registered entity where export is set, in a structured, machine-readable form.
    3. Erasure: Erase(subject) is the delete transition above, applied to the subject's rows in every registered entity per their strategies, reporting which entities were erased and which retained rows (with the retention reason).
    4. Restriction: archiving a subject, plus a flag that processing is restricted for the applications that need to honor it.
    5. Rectification: ordinary edits.
    6. Erasure is orchestrated, not assumed. Products store their own data; each registers an eraser for its entities (or relies on annotations for simple cases). Erase runs them, records per-entity results without personal data, and can be resumed after a partial failure.
    7. Retention is a policy input, not a platform constant. SystemForge records retention per field and per entity (decision 24) and runs the purge, dormancy and offload operations below, but does not fix periods: they follow the product's tier and packaging, which the application configures.
    8. Erasure can be held. A subject may be covered by one or more legal holds (ADR-012). While any hold covers it, Erase, the delete transition, purge and edits to pii fields refuse with ErrHeld, and archive remains available. Holds are placed and released by a legal manager, not by a platform administrator acting alone. ADR-012 defines holds, what they preserve, and how they are enforced. SystemForge models the hold and its custodians; legal case management stays outside it.
    9. The audit log is exportable. Lifecycle transitions, erasure results and hold changes are recorded without personal data, and an administrator can export them (by organization and time range), so a customer can evidence what was erased, when and under whose authority. This is independent of the hold: the export serves accountability, the hold serves refusal.
    10. Managed principals are covered. A managed subject's request is made by an authorized manager, and the audit record names that manager's authority; once released, the principal acts for itself.
    11. A request is verified before it is honored. Verification of the requester's identity is the application's responsibility; SystemForge exposes the operations behind the authorization checks of decision 5.

    Release of a managed principal (amends ADR-009, decision 3)¶

    1. Release is a record with a state, not an instant. Releasing a principal creates a release record in state open. While it is open:
      • the principal is self-managed: it may act for itself, and the managers' authority over it is suspended (managed is false);
      • the manager relationships are retained, dormant, so a mistaken release can be reversed;
      • the record may be edited (for example its note and effective details) and may be deleted, which restores the management arrangement.
    2. Closing a release is the formal end of management. Closing removes the manager relationships and cannot be undone by a manager. After a release is closed only a platform administrator may change or reopen it.
    3. ADR-009's other rules stand: a principal without a verified email must have a manager, so release still requires a verified email, and email remains independent of management.

    Dead accounts, purge and storage tiering¶

    1. An account that has not proven itself is provisional. A principal created by a signup that has not yet verified an email or taken a first meaningful action is provisional. A deployment sets the graduation criterion and a short time to live. A provisional account that does not graduate within it is purged (decision 20), with no tombstone. Spam that never graduates therefore never becomes an identity record that other records reference. A deployment may further restrict what a provisional account can do (for example, it holds no roles and is not referenced as an author), so that graduation is the point at which other records begin to depend on it.
    2. purge removes a subject's rows when everything that depends on them can also be removed. It differs from deleted in that no row, and no personal data, remains. A subject is purgeable when every record that references it, directly or transitively, is itself purgeable: it belongs to the same subject, is declared cascade (below), and is not covered by a hold. Otherwise the purge is refused with a dependency report and the caller may fall back to the ordinary delete, which keeps a tombstone. The report names the entity, the count and the reason for each blocker and never the contents of another subject's rows.

    3. Relationships carry a purge disposition in the catalog. Each edge between registered entities is declared:

      Disposition Meaning Example
      cascade The dependent is owned by the subject and is purged with it Sessions, API keys, preferences, memberships of the subject's personal organization
      detach The dependent stays and its reference is cleared or replaced by a placeholder A comment authored in another organization's workspace
      restrict The dependent blocks the purge Billing records, a membership in an organization that still exists, another subject's data

      An edge without a disposition is an error at registration, as an unclassified field is. Where a deployment's product has content shared with other subjects, which of restrict and detach applies is the product's decision. 22. Purge is orchestrated by the platform, not by database cascades. Executing a purge marks the subject purging, which the enforcement hook (ADR-012) uses to refuse new dependent writes, then removes dependents leaf first in batches that can be resumed, and finally the subject. Foreign keys on registered entities stay RESTRICT, as a safety net that makes an undeclared dependency fail loudly. Every step runs through the same hook, so hold checks, before-images and audit apply; the audit record carries counts and no personal data. A versioned entity's before-images are part of the closure and are purged with it unless a hold covers the subject. 23. Purge leaves an abuse fingerprint, not personal data. So a purged account is not simply recreated, the platform may retain a keyed hash of the verified email (and, if the deployment chooses, network indicators) under a key managed as ADR-013 decides. The fingerprint cannot be reversed, is classified in the catalog, has its own retention period, and is used only to block re-registration and to measure abuse. The lawful basis and the period are the deployment's decision and need review for its jurisdictions. 24. Dormant accounts follow a ladder, and retention is structured. - An account records when it was last active, updated at most once a day so activity does not become a write on every request. - A deployment sets, per tier, a dormancy period after which the owner is notified, then the account is archived (decision 2), then deleted after a further period, and purged where decision 20 allows. - Accounts with active billing, a covering hold, or the sole ownership of an organization that has other members are excluded until that is resolved. - The notice tells the owner how to keep the account: signing in before archive resets the period, and an archived account is restored on request. - Retention in the catalog is structured as keep (a duration) and then (purge or offload), per entity, with the periods supplied by configuration (decision 11). 25. Archive in storage terms is "offload", and it is a separate tier. archived (decision 2) is a visibility state and stays in the database. Moving rows out of the primary database is offload, defined as: - Data eligible for offload is historical and cold: tombstones past their period, before-images of versioned entities, snapshots whose holds were released, and old audit records. - Offloaded rows are written to the snapshot store (ADR-012, decision 13), a database table set or an S3-compatible object store with Parquet files, with a manifest recording the catalog version, row counts and digest, and a stub left where a reference requires one. - Before-image and audit storage is partitioned by time from the first version, so that cold partitions can be detached, exported and purged without rewriting live tables. Hot queries use partial indexes and default scopes that exclude archived and deleted rows. - Offload is not destruction, so it is allowed under a hold, but it is a custody change and is audited. Offloaded data stays reachable by subject for export, erasure and hold: encryption under per-subject keys (ADR-013) lets erasure destroy a subject's key and cover the offloaded copy without rewriting files. - Purge is the only deletion. It is explicit, retention-driven and hold-aware.

    Consequences¶

    • Accounts can be hidden without losing history, and an administrator can restore them.
    • Deletion never breaks references, and the personal data in a deleted row is gone by construction rather than by convention.
    • One registry answers "where do we keep personal data?" for the platform and for every product built on it, and CI keeps it complete.
    • Products take on work: annotate their entities and register erasers. The cost is small for simple tables and explicit for the rest.
    • Release becomes reversible until it is closed. The cost is a second state and a dormant-relationship rule that every authority check must honor, including the managed claim.
    • ADR-009's "release removes managers" becomes "closing removes managers". Code and docs that equate release with removal change.
    • Spam and abandoned accounts do not accumulate. Provisional accounts expire without a trace, the dormancy ladder reclaims the rest, and purge is safe because it refuses whatever still has dependents.
    • Every relationship needs a disposition. Adding an edge between registered entities now carries a decision, which is the same discipline as classifying a field and is enforced the same way.
    • Large histories have somewhere to go. Time-partitioned history and the offload tier bound the growth of the primary database, at the cost of a second place to look when data is subject to a request or a hold.
    • ADR-011's earlier "no hard delete" is withdrawn. Row removal exists, as purge, but only where the dependency rule allows it.

    Open questions¶

    • Retention periods, which follow product tier and packaging decisions.
    • Whether organizations get the same Erase semantics for their member data, or only archive and delete of the organization record.
    • The shape of the export format (one archive per subject, with a manifest).
    • What counts as graduating from provisional: a verified email, a first meaningful action, or either.
    • The retention period and lawful basis for the abuse fingerprint, which need legal review per jurisdiction.
    • Whether an account under investigation needs a suspended state between active and purge, or whether archive covers it.
    • The default disposition for content shared with other subjects, restrict or detach, per product.
    • The dormancy periods per tier, which follow packaging.
